## Complex and map

K is the abstract simplicial complex generated by the four-label sets of the PR241 tetrahedra. All their faces are included; by definition two abstract simplices meet along their common labels. The labels v, f and c are disjoint. The new check requires the tetrahedra to be unique and the triangle incidences to be one or two, with opposite orientations. B is the subcomplex generated by the triangles with a single incidence. It is required to be identical to the PR243 boundary, including the PR240 caps.

For arbitrary v in the prescribed closed domain, F_v is affine on each tetrahedron, given by its four PR241 positions. The exact sharing of the coordinate functions makes it continuous on K. The inherited strictly positive six-volumes make each affine restriction injective. Global injectivity is not yet assumed, and K is not assumed to be a manifold from its Euler characteristic alone.

## Local positive-chain lemma (dimensions two or three)

Let x be a point of |K| outside |B|, and sigma its unique minimal carrier simplex (x is in its relative interior). Consider the chain of all maximal simplices containing sigma. Every facet containing sigma has two opposite incidences in this chain: the second incidence also contains sigma. A boundary facet containing sigma would place x in B, which is excluded. The residual boundary is therefore carried by facets that do not contain sigma.

The image of x belongs to none of these residual facets. Indeed, each such facet belongs to a maximal simplex containing x, on which F_v is injective. The family is finite: there is an open ball around F_v(x) disjoint from this residual boundary. No radius uniform in v is needed; v has been fixed arbitrarily, endpoints included.

In this ball, the multiplicity of the local chain, outside the image facets, is constant. To see this, join two generic points by a generic polygonal path that stays in the ball. The jumps are the oriented coefficients of the boundary; they are zero. Coincident planes are grouped and the crossings avoid the codimension-two intersections. This is the same elementary chain argument as PR243, applied to a finite subchain. In dimension two, replace facets/planes by edges/lines.

This constant is at least one: the interior of any incident simplex contains points arbitrarily close to F_v(x), and all simplices have positive orientation. So an abstract preimage outside B forces a positive multiplicity on a WHOLE ball of generic points, even if x lies on an edge or a vertex.

## Injectivity outside B and separation of the boundary

PR243 gives global multiplicity one in Ω and zero outside, away from the facets, with closed union exactly the closure of Ω. If two distinct abstract points x,z outside B had the same image, no maximal simplex could contain both, by affine injectivity. Their two families of incident simplices would therefore be disjoint. The two local lemmas would force a global multiplicity of at least two on a common ball, a contradiction.

A point outside B cannot have its image on S=F_v(B) either: its local ball contains generic points outside Ω, since S is the embedded PR243 boundary surface, whereas the global multiplicity there is zero. Thus F_v is injective on |K|\|B| and its image there is disjoint from S.

## Injectivity of B, caps included

The sides are injective down to their edge/vertex labels by the exact PR243 traversal. For a cap, apply the lemma in dimension two to its triangle complex, after choosing the positive orientation in its plane. The new one/two incidences must give exactly the two PR240 loops as abstract boundary. The positive areas, the oriented cancellation and the multiplicity one on the annulus are inherited from PR240. The lemma excludes two interior preimages and excludes an interior point mapping onto the loops, since every point of these loops has points outside the annulus arbitrarily close to it. The two loops themselves are injective and disjoint by their strict convexity and their strict PR240 inclusion. The cap is therefore injective on its whole closed complex.

The two caps lie in distinct planes. PR243 establishes that the sides meet these planes exactly at the fixed loops, with the same labels and coordinates. The new checks identify these loops as common subcomplexes, not only as sets equal in position. The injectivity of each piece therefore implies that of the whole glued B. It is not necessary to assume a sphere, nor to remove the boundaries.

## Contacts of all dimensions

The three steps give the injectivity of F_v on the whole of |K|. For all subcomplexes A,C, F_v(|A|) ∩ F_v(|C|) = F_v(|A∩C|): every common point has a unique preimage, which belongs to both. Apply this to the subcomplexes of the PR241 cells. Their c labels are specific to each cell and their f labels are shared exactly when they have the same source face. An intersection without a common source vertex is therefore impossible. The allowed contacts are precisely the shared abstract simplices, with no additional point or line contact between non-incident cells.

The proof holds for EACH v of the closed domain by the same positivity certificates; no value of v was chosen for the application, and no geometric or parametric subdivision is performed. This result concerns the PR241 realization only. It does not prove a surface equality with another native triangulation, an equality of centers, or an OpenFOAM admission.

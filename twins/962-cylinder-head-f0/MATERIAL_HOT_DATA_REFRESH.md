## HT1: heat treatment changes the hot-strength ranking

[Lenoir et al. (2025), Materials Science and Engineering A 948, 149372](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msea.2025.149372) compares commercial Aheadd HT1 after stress relief at 300 °C for four hours with separately aged states. The authors report approximately:

| Test temperature | Strain rate | Stress-relieved yield | After additional ageing at 400 °C for one or four hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 °C | 0.01 per second | 230 MPa | 150–160 MPa |
| 250 °C | 0.00001 per second | 200 MPa | 120–130 MPa |

These are grouped approximate prose values, not extracted raw curves, fitted laws or design allowables. The hot-strength ordering differs from room-temperature ordering. The study's schedules must remain separate from the manufacturer's unidentified treatments #1 and #2. Figure 6 and supplementary Figure S5 identify the next numerical-data target; complete specimen, orientation and treatment records must accompany it.

## Separate 2026 prototype leads

[Lenoir et al. (2026), MSEA 150568](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msea.2026.150568) studies an as-printed Al–Fe–Cr–Mn–Zr research prototype. SIMaP calls the Al-2Fe-2Cr-1Mn-0.7Zr wt.% prototype HT2. The paper's indexed title/abstract use wt.% while its indexed conclusion uses at.%; the paper's composition basis and measured composition remain unresolved. This institutional name does not identify a commercial HT1, CP1 or retained EOS/Nikon recipe.

Publisher-indexed prose reports approximately 210 MPa yield at 100 °C/0.01 per second, 190 MPa at 250 °C/0.00001 per second and 125 MPa at 350 °C/0.00001 per second. These isolated observations have different test rates. They are neither a constant-rate curve nor design allowables. Complete loading orientation, specimen, dwell, scatter and raw curve records were not acquired.

A separate [Additive Manufacturing study, 105307](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addma.2026.105307) reports processing-window and productivity observations for modified Al research alloys. Its highlighted Cr-containing prototype is separate from the retained production states. Reduced conductivity can aid melt-pool processing; it does not establish cylinder-head heat removal, strength or a calibrated LPBF thermal law. Indexed data availability is on request; no request was sent.

Both 2026 leads were inspected through publisher-indexed text. Direct publisher opens returned HTTP 403; no full paper, figure, supplement or raw dataset was downloaded. No coefficients, constitutive cards or production selection changed.

## CuCrZr: thermal data from another manufacturing state

[Biffi et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40516-023-00240-7) measured thermal behavior over 25–300 °C after a modified Sharebot process, solution treatment at 1,000 °C for one hour with water quenching, then ageing at 580 °C for five hours. Conductivity uncertainty is reported as 7%. Its paper-labelled XZ conductivity is approximately 350 W/(m K); XY decreases from approximately 345 to 300 W/(m K). The specimen and heat-flow axes must be checked before any tensor interpretation.

That process and treatment differ from both retained EOS recipes. These measurements support a separate research comparison; they cannot replace the EOS states' missing thermal laws or hot strength.

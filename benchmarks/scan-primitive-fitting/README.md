# Scan-to-CAD plane fitting: tool and witness

Fitting CAD features to a scan had no assigned tool. The 935 head twin used a
hand-written level detector that bins horizontal facets in Z. With a 0.1 mm
window it found levels near only 7 of 28 fin faces on 2026-11-09, because cast
fin faces are drafted by 1–2° and smear over 1–2 mm of height. The twin's later
per-face fit recovered all 28 on the real scan; it is not on main and has no
witness. The negative control below is the fine-level detector.

**Assigned tool:** Open3D 0.19 `segment_plane`, run repeatedly. Each round
keeps only the RANSAC inliers whose normals agree with the plane (within 15°)
and refits the plane by least squares. Set the distance threshold to about
three times the scan noise.

## Witness

[`witness_drafted_fins.py`](witness_drafted_fins.py) builds a stack of 12
annular fins (24 faces, 40–80 mm radius, 3 mm thick, 7 mm pitch). Each face
carries a known draft of 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 or 3° at a random azimuth. The
faces are meshed at 1 mm with 0.03 mm normal noise. The ground truth is
generated by the script, never by a detector. The acceptance (at least 95 % of
faces within 0.1° tilt and 0.05 mm offset, no spurious planes) and the RANSAC
parameters were fixed before each method's first run.

Result ([`results-20261110.json`](results-20261110.json), reproduced
byte-identically on a second run):

| Detector | Faces within acceptance | Spurious | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open3D sequential RANSAC + normal agreement | 24 / 24, every draft angle | 0 | **passed** |
| Open3D `detect_planar_patches`, library defaults | 2 / 24 (the outermost faces) | 0 | failed |
| Z-level histogram (the 935 twin's method) | 4 / 24 (undrafted faces only) | 1,514 | failed |

Largest errors of the passing method: 0.0011° in tilt and 0.0006 mm in offset.

## Run it

```bash
uv venv --python 3.12 work/scan-primitive-fitting-venv
uv pip install --python work/scan-primitive-fitting-venv/bin/python \
  --require-hashes -r benchmarks/scan-primitive-fitting/requirements.lock.txt
work/scan-primitive-fitting-venv/bin/python -I \
  benchmarks/scan-primitive-fitting/witness_drafted_fins.py --output <new.json>
```

About 30 s and under 3 GB. The lock pins Open3D 0.19.0 and NumPy 2.2.6 with
hashes, and resolves for `manylinux_2_31` x86-64. The `recon` image also
carries Open3D 0.19.0, but installed unpinned and with no lock, so it is not
used here.

## What this does not establish

This qualifies plane detection on synthetic drafted faces. It says nothing
about the accuracy of any real scan, part or fit. Cylinders, cones, tori and
fillets are not covered: CGAL's shape detection (header-only C++, in the
`analysis-cgal-toolchain` recipe) is the candidate there, and has no witness
yet. On a real scan, check each fitted plane against an independent deviation
measure, not against the detector's own residual.

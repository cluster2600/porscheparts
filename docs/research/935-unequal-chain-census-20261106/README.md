rejects global planar caps on the dominant unresolved large contours. This
experiment follows that evidence and the
[scan-repair workflow](../../architecture/scan-repair-workflow.md). It tests a

coordinates must be distinct, all three owners must be acquired raw facets,

four-corner proposal renamed after a refusal. No source edge is split and no

## Two preserved normal-association models

The [first producer](source/census_unequal_chains.py) and
[checker](source/verify_unequal_chains.py) require every triangle to match all
three class support directions. The result is retained without in-place edits.

A separate literal curved strip demonstrates a limitation of that uniform
cone: each source normal is perpendicular to its own acquired edge, and each
realised triangle matches its own border normal above 0.8, but a triangle need
not match the normals of other borders on the curved patch. This is a model
counterexample, not permission to loosen a numerical threshold.

The [versioned local-border producer](source/census_unequal_chains_v2.py) and
[independent checker](source/verify_unequal_chains_v2.py) therefore test an
explicitly different association rule: every triangle must own exactly one
actual acquired border edge and meet the unchanged 0.8 direction bound against
that edge's actual class direction. Any triangulation with an unsupported
interior triangle or multiple source edges on a triangle is excluded. All
other geometric, material-side and conflict bounds remain fixed.

Both actual complete replays find zero eligible chains. Their saved census
arrays are byte-identical **on this input** because scope, keys, neighbourhoods
and empty eligible arrays coincide. The models are not mathematically identical:
the independent curved fixture distinguishes them. No native failed proposal is
retried and neither model grants scan-construction admission.

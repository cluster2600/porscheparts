# Documented materials of 993 and 935 systems

Research rechecked on 3 October 2026 in German, English and French. Original,
replacement and future improved part materials have distinct identities.
“Aluminium” or “magnesium” denotes an alloy family here, without a certificate
or design allowables.

| Assembly and part | Documented material | Evidence scope |
|---|---|---|
| Factory 935: metal horizontal drive housings | Magnesium | Jim Torres reports observations of factory housings and his own racing failures. No precise grade, reference or model year. |
| Jim Torres horizontal reproduction: drive housings | Cast aluminium | Manufacturer statement about its product. |
| Jim Torres reproduction: rotating rotor | CNC machined aluminium 7075 | Manufacturer statement; metallurgical condition unspecified, so do not invent T 6. |
| Design911 reproduction `93510610300R/1`: rotor and funnel | Aluminium rotor; GRP glass/resin composite funnel | Data for this reproduction; composite duct is distinct from metal drive housing. |
| Period naturally aspirated 964/993 Carrera: rotor and housing | Cast magnesium | Lee Rice workshop account in PANDO; reported identification without chemical analysis. |
| 964/993 Turbo: eleven straight-blade rotor and housing | Aluminium according to Lee Rice | Same workshop account; confirm for a precise reference and specimen. |
| Carrera replacement `96410601531` currently sold by Partworks as a new Porsche part | Advertised aluminium | Direct product description; establishes neither all period materials nor a Porsche change date. |

## Application to both OBJ files and calculations

Wolfe descriptions of the [rotor](https://www.wolfeclassics.com/shop/p/porsche-935-fan-3d-scan)
and [drive](https://www.wolfeclassics.com/shop/p/porsche-935-fan-drive-3d-scan)
do not declare material. Torres documents factory housing magnesium; this does
not identify the scanned 935 rotor as magnesium. His observation about early
934 aluminium rotors does not identify this 935 rotor. Internal parts, shafts,
gears and bearings need their own identification.

The earlier “material unknown” answer concerned specimens but omitted these
documentable historical and reproduction materials. AlSi10Mg in the 993
programme is an additive candidate, not identification of the original Porsche
alloy.

Specimen material fields remain unassigned pending each part's identification.
Comparative aluminium/magnesium scenarios may be explicitly hypothetical with
their own grade, process and condition. Generic density must not become measured
mass; machined 7075 properties do not qualify printed AlSi10Mg. Prepared Wolfe
questions must obtain markings, provenance, masses and material data for both
parts.

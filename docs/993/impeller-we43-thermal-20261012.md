# WE43 impeller: exploratory transient build thermal study

Part: `993-ENG-COOLING-IMPELLER-WE43-F1-0001`.

This study computes transient bulk heat transfer during the LPBF build of the
existing concept impeller. It uses the actual admitted STL, with all 1,867 flat
or 6,286 tilted physical 30 µm part slices. The user has no selected machine or machine parameter file;
the reference recipe and missing calibration are therefore explicit.

The earlier [layer-stack preview](../../parts/993-eng-cooling-impeller-we43-f1-0001/media/print-simulation/build.gif)
is a geometric animation. It does not contain a thermal calculation. Its generic
support proxy is not a supplier support design. Correction PR #167 was merged
into `main` at `0cbee3710ea6b23f210e872011f8a7f5452e6676`.

## Orientation decision

The flat `build_z` orientation, with the cup open upwards, is now the preferred
**next study candidate**. It reduces part height from 188.56 to 56 mm and physical
part layers from 6,286 to 1,867. At the assumed 8 s recoating time, the reduction
is 9.82 h. This is a geometric/timing result, not a supplier production quote.
The original 45° selection minimized downward projected surface area only; it
was not a thermal or manufacturing optimization.

The flat calculation uses the same metal, laser recipe, absorption, plate,
6 mm standoff and homogenized support assumptions. Its 2 mm / eight-substep
case estimates 23.69 h total and 65.76 cm³ of support metal. These support
columns have not been designed for manufacture or removal. The entire part
still spans 243.0 × 244.8 mm in XY; no machine clearance is qualified.

Metal supports can provide heat transfer and mechanical restraint, rather than
only holding geometric overhangs; [EOS describes these roles](https://www.eos.info/content/blog/know-how/support-free).
That general explanation does not qualify a WE43 machine recipe or this support
layout. The lower build is a reason to prioritize flat trials, not permission
to omit every support.

temperature. Values beyond the property range are unsuitable as predictions.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|

| Flat | 2 mm / 4 | 23.69 | 65.76 | 53.62 | no |
| Flat | 3 mm / 4 | 23.79 | 67.30 | 54.15 | no |

At 2 mm, four versus eight substeps changes the flat peak by 1.22 K but the tilted
peak by 411.48 K. The tilted result is not temporally converged and all its peaks
exceed the material property's reference range. Those values demonstrate a model
limitation, not a predicted molten or failed impeller. Two grids and two time
resolutions are sensitivity checks, not proof of convergence for either case.

At the matched 2 mm / eight-substep setting, flat versus tilted changes the
modelled build estimate from 48.92 to 23.69 h and the assumed support metal from
303.93 to 65.76 cm³. These numbers depend on the simplified support columns and
scan/recoat schedule. They support studying the flat orientation next, while
leaving its manufacturing support design open.

## Equations and discretization

[The calculation](../../parts/993-eng-cooling-impeller-we43-f1-0001/source/simulate_lpbf_thermal.py)
reuses the repository's backward-Euler sparse conjugate-gradient thermal step.
For each active cell it solves, in SI units:

```text
C_i (T_i(new) - T_i(old)) / dt
  + sum_j G_ij (T_i(new) - T_j(new))
  + H_i T_i(new) = Q_i + H_i T_bath
C_i = rho cp dx^3 f_i
G_ij = k dx 2 f_i f_j / (f_i + f_j)
```

Binary occupancy is first sampled at 1 mm. Groups of these samples form 2 or
3 mm thermal cells, preserving metal volume and heat capacity exactly relative
to that sampling. Conductance uses the harmonic mean of adjacent solid
fractions; it does not resolve subcell blade paths or contact areas. Convection
and radiation act on the exposed coarse-cell face area. This approximation
requires refinement for predictive thin-wall temperatures.

Cells activate progressively in Z. New cells start at the assumed plate
reference temperature. Supports occupy columns below the first part cell at each
XY position and use an effective solid fraction of 0.15 for capacity,
conductance and scan energy. This is an assumed thermal connection, not a
manufacturable support layout. Plate attachment is perfect contact to an
isothermal reservoir. All other exposed faces exchange heat with ambient gas by
convection and linearized radiation; surrounding powder conduction is omitted.

Each macro layer alternates heating and recoating substeps. The deposited laser
energy is integrated from the physical slice areas using `P / (v h t)` and the
assumed absorption; support energy uses the homogenized support solid volume.
Heating is distributed over the current macro layer. Individual tracks, contours,
jumps and the moving laser spot are unresolved. All cells of a macro layer are
activated together; time refinement does not eliminate this spatial lumping.

The calculation checks the CAD checksum, manifold solid, full slice sequence,
slice volume against CAD (0.1% tolerance), voxel volume against CAD (2% tolerance),
finite solved temperatures, iterative convergence and each step's energy balance
(relative tolerance 1e-6). Input hashes are frozen at start and rechecked before
publication; field and history hashes are included in the summary.

## Mesh admission and rejected runs

| Geometry sampling | Part volume error vs CAD | Decision |
|---|---:|---|
| 2 mm | −4.022% | rejected before thermal solve |
| 1.5 mm | −4.816% | rejected before thermal solve |
| 1 mm | +0.247% | admitted, aggregated into 2 and 3 mm thermal cells |

Volume agreement is an admission check, not evidence of spatial convergence or
accurate blade temperatures. Thin features and heat paths remain voxelized.
A spatially converged prediction has not been established. The binary 1 mm
thermal runs were stopped for computational cost before completion, with no
final result retained. Partial-volume thermal cells reduce the number of
unknowns while keeping the admitted metal volume. Sensitivity runs compare
2 and 3 mm thermal cells, and four versus eight scan/recoat substeps.

Early development runs used an incorrectly translated ray-classification frame.
They are excluded in full, including their temperature fields. The defect was
fixed by translating all three mesh minima to zero before voxel classification;
a regression checks translation invariance and full solid volume. An initial
2 mm odd-intersection rejection made before that fix is superseded by the
corrected-frame volume audit above.

  --output work/we43-flat

## Numerical verification

Twelve focused tests pass locally with the numerical dependencies installed:
analytical one-cell heating/cooling, two-cell conduction, translated geometry
and conserved volume, invalid-input rejection, renderer checksum rejection,
independent energy integration from the six saved fields, input/output
provenance and the polymer export's separate scope. Maximum step energy residuals
are below 1e-6 and whole-build residuals below 1e-3 J in every saved case.
The export was also reloaded and checked for closed topology, volume preservation
and the expected transformed bounds. These are numerical and file checks;
there is no physical print or machine calibration.

## Interpretation limits

This is an actual numerical bulk heat-transfer calculation, with numerical
verification distinct from experimental validation. Constant wrought-alloy
properties, assumed thermal supports and missing powder conduction limit its
physical accuracy. Exceeding 100 °C also exceeds the reference heat-capacity
range and must be flagged, not treated as a qualified temperature prediction.

Melt-pool temperature and shape, melting/solidification enthalpy, evaporation,
porosity, residual stress, warping and support-release distortion are not solved.
There is no machine calibration or coupon validation. The calculation cannot
establish print success, support removal access, dimensional accuracy or rotor
safety. Those require a machine-specific process/material card, measured
interfaces, designed supports, convergence studies and physical validation.
